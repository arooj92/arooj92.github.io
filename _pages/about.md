I am an HCI researcher at the Lifestyle Computing Lab, Keio University, where I design and study **tangible interfaces for STEM learning** — physical, hands-on systems that make abstract concepts in geometry and structural engineering accessible to children.

My doctoral work introduced TIEboard and TangiBuild, two tangible learning tools that blend physical manipulation with digital feedback to support geometric thinking and collaborative exploration in K–12 settings.

I received my M.S. from Keio Media Design (2022), where I participated in an exchange program with Stanford University, and my Ph.D. from Keio University (2026). I am currently a Research Assistant at the Lifestyle Computing Lab.
